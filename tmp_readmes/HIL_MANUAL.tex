\section{The system in one page}

Three containers and a browser. Each owns exactly one thing.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.6cm}p{11.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{\texttt{ai\_worker}} & The hardware. Leader arms, follower arms, head,
lift, swerve base, four cameras. Publishes joint states, consumes trajectory
commands. Knows nothing about policies or datasets.\\[1mm]
\textbf{\texttt{groot\_server}} & The policy. GR00T-N1.7 behind a TensorRT DiT.
Reads observations, writes arm commands. \textbf{16 dimensions --- both arms
only}; head, lift and base are never commanded by it.\\[1mm]
\textbf{\texttt{cyclo\_intelligence}} & Orchestration. Session state machine,
recording, conversion, the HTTP/WebSocket surface the browser talks to.\\[1mm]
\textbf{browser} & The operator's Online-RL page. Start/stop the policy, take
over, label subtasks, watch the correction counter.\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\good{\textbf{The one fact that explains most of the design.} The teleoperation
node and the policy publish to the \emph{same} topic --- the arm command
broadcaster. There is no separate ``policy channel''. That is why a recording is
valid whether a human or the policy was driving, and it is why a latched
``policy is active'' flag has to exist to stop the two of them writing at once.}

\subsection{What a session looks like}

You start the policy. It runs. When it does something wrong you take over, fix
it, and hand back. Those correction frames are the training signal --- that is
HG-DAgger. The whole system exists to make that loop safe, and to get the
corrections into a dataset with a label saying which frames were yours.

\section{Operating a session}

\subsection{Bring-up order}

Follower, then leader, then the bridge. The order matters: the bridge subscribes
to topics the other two create.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=sh]
# 1. follower -- the physical arms/head/lift you are controlling
ros2 launch ffw_bringup ffw_sg2_follower_ai.launch.py

# 2. leader -- the two hand controllers
ros2 launch ffw_bringup ffw_a2_leader_ai.launch.py

# 3. the bridge that makes the pause/resume gesture work
python3 leader_bridge.py
\end{lstlisting}

\note{\textbf{Check it actually worked, do not assume.} The leader and follower
can both stay up through a whole inference session --- they do not contend,
because T14 silences the leader chain while the policy is driving. If the arms
fight, T14 is not arriving.}

\subsection{The takeover loop}
\label{sec:loop}

Assume the policy is mid-rollout.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=8mm]
\item \textbf{Joystick drag-down, hold ${\geq}1$\,s} --- the policy stops.
      INFERENCING $\to$ PAUSED.
\item \textbf{Press tact} on the arm you want --- it engages. You are driving;
      these frames are the training signal.
\item \textbf{Press tact again} --- it disengages. The gripper holds its
      teleoped value.
\item \textbf{Joystick drag-down, hold ${\geq}1$\,s} --- the policy resumes.
      PAUSED $\to$ INFERENCING.
\end{enumerate}

\good{\textbf{Engage does not sweep.} SG2 runs control mode 2 (relative delta).
On engage it captures a leader anchor and a follower anchor and commands
\\[1mm]\hspace*{4mm}\texttt{goal = follower\_anchor + (leader\_now - leader\_anchor)}\\[1mm]
so nothing jumps even if leader and follower are far apart. Mode 1 (absolute
MoveJ) \emph{does} sweep, which is why \texttt{slow\_start} is enabled there and
disabled in mode 2.}

Two things to internalise:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The tact is a toggle.} Same button engages and stops.
\item \textbf{The drag gesture is one topic with two meanings.} It publishes a
bare \texttt{std\_msgs/Empty}; the orchestrator decides pause-vs-resume from the
phase it is currently in.
\end{itemize}

The gaps between steps 1$\to$2 and 3$\to$4 --- policy paused, no arm engaged,
nobody driving --- are labelled $-1$ and excluded from training. That is correct.
You do not need to rush.

\subsection{The 40-frame rule}

\warn{\textbf{A correction shorter than 40 frames produces zero training
samples.} A training sample is a 40-frame window (one GR00T action chunk), not a
frame. A run of 45 frames yields $45-40+1 = 6$ windows; a run of 120 yields 81.
\textbf{Sample count is strongly nonlinear in takeover length}, so a few long
corrections are worth far more than many short nudges. The UI shows a live
counter against this threshold --- watch it.}

At 30\,Hz, 40 frames is 1.33\,s. In practice, aim for corrections of several
seconds.

\subsection{What not to do}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Do not reach for the tact while the policy is INFERENCING} to
      trigger a recording. On this robot the tact's recording trigger is
      disabled (\texttt{record\_triggers\_enabled: false}) precisely because one
      press also engages that arm --- and an engaged leader publishes to the same
      topic the policy is driving.
\item \textbf{Do not save an episode without an outcome.} HG-DAgger aggregation
      defaults to successful episodes only; a failed episode's human frames are
      corrections toward an outcome that never worked.
\end{itemize}

gate must refuse a declared dimension whose \texttt{q99}$-$\texttt{q01} or

\section{The intervention label}
\label{sec:defect}

\subsection{How it is computed}

\texttt{/task/inference\_status} publishes \texttt{inference\_phase} on
transitions only, so the converter forward-fills it onto the frame grid.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=py,caption={\texttt{base\_converter.py} --- \texttt{\_assign\_intervention\_flags}, semantics}]
INFERENCING                            ->  0   # the policy drove
PAUSED + teleop engaged + not handoff  ->  1   # the human drove
PAUSED + leader NOT engaged            -> -1   # nobody driving (your step 1->2 gap)
handoff transient                      -> -1
before the first status message        -> -1   # phase genuinely unobserved
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The defect}

\warn{\textbf{\texttt{to\_lerobot\_v30.py} writes the label.
\texttt{to\_lerobot\_v21.py} does not. Isaac-GR00T requires v2.1.}\\[1.5mm]
So every dataset exported for GR00T training silently loses the human/policy
label --- and \texttt{reward}, \texttt{done} and \texttt{sample\_weight} with it.
Downstream, the split tool refuses to run, and the corrections become
indistinguishable from the policy's own behaviour.}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=py,caption={The fix --- mirror this block into \texttt{to\_lerobot\_v21.py}}]
# Online-RL columns, present only when /task/inference_status was in the bag.
# Isaac-GR00T requires v2.1, so without this every HG-DAgger export loses the
# human/policy label and degrades to ordinary behaviour cloning.
has_hil_feature = any(ep.intervention_flags for ep in episodes)
if has_hil_feature:
    schema_fields.append(pa.field("intervention",  pa.int64()))
    schema_fields.append(pa.field("reward",        pa.int64()))
    schema_fields.append(pa.field("done",          pa.bool_()))
    schema_fields.append(pa.field("sample_weight", pa.float32()))
\end{lstlisting}

The matching array fill and the \texttt{hf\_features} entry are also needed ---
\textbf{declaring it in \texttt{meta/info.json} is not optional}, because tooling
that reads the feature list will not see a column that exists only in the parquet.

\subsection{Polarity --- the silent one}

\warn{\texttt{intervention} is \textbf{1 = human, 0 = policy, $-1$ = excluded}.
Some downstream tooling instead expects \texttt{task\_is\_policy}, which is
\textbf{1 = policy, 0 = human}. \textbf{These are inverses.} Emit one name, state
the polarity in \texttt{info.json}, and make consumers assert on the name.
Getting it wrong raises nothing --- the run simply trains on the exact inverse of
what was intended.}

\subsection{Until it is fixed}

\good{Convert \textbf{both} formats and keep the v3.0 copy. It is the only export
that carries the labels. If you already have a v2.1-only export, the labels can
be reconstructed --- see Section~\ref{sec:recover} --- but that is inference, not
ground truth.}

\section{Recovering labels from an unlabelled export}
\label{sec:recover}

If a session was exported without the intervention column, the physical
signature of each takeover still survives in the joint trajectories.

\subsection{Method}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=8mm]
\item \textbf{Find the pauses.} A takeover is bracketed by two stops --- it takes
      time to grab the leader and time to hand back. Flag runs where total joint
      speed stays under a threshold.
\item \textbf{Pair them.} The span between two consecutive stops inside one
      subtask is a candidate correction.
\item \textbf{Confirm with the policy itself.} Run the base checkpoint over
      frames inside and outside the candidates and compare its prediction to what
      was recorded.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lstlisting}[style=py,caption={Stop detection --- the only step that needs no model}]
FPS = 30
S = np.stack(df["observation.state"].to_numpy())[:, :16]     # both arms
v = np.linalg.norm(np.diff(S, axis=0), axis=1) * FPS * 180/np.pi   # deg/s
v = np.concatenate([[v[0]], v])
still = v < 0.8                       # deg/s
# keep runs of >= 12 frames (0.4 s); the span BETWEEN two such runs,
# inside one subtask, is a candidate takeover
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{Why the third step is the one that proves it}

The policy is a free, calibrated detector of its own behaviour. On frames it
generated it predicts them back to within its normal open-loop error. On frames a
human generated it cannot --- if it could have produced that motion, there would
have been no reason to intervene.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{disagreement} & \\
\midrule
outside candidate spans & 1.59$^\circ$ & the policy reproducing itself\\
inside candidate spans  & 3.13$^\circ$ & the policy failing to reproduce a human\\
\midrule
\multicolumn{3}{@{}l}{ratio 1.96$\times$, $p \approx 9\times10^{-21}$, and it holds separately in every subtask}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\note{\textbf{Do not use smoothness as the test.} ``The careful parts are
smoother'' is true whether or not a human is driving --- it is near-circular. The
disagreement test does not depend on it.}

\section{Training}

\subsection{The three-way split}

Isaac-GR00T has no per-frame loss weighting, so the frame-level scheme (human
2.0 / auto 0.3 / pre-intervention 0.0) has to be reproduced at the \emph{dataset}
level via \texttt{mix\_ratio}. The split is done at export time.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{dataset} & \textbf{contents} & \textbf{episodes} & \textbf{frames}\\
\midrule
original       & the base demonstrations       & 31 & 55{,}701\\
\texttt{dagger\_human} & the operator's corrections only & 39 & 6{,}765\\
\texttt{dagger\_auto}  & the policy's own frames between them & 52 & 31{,}804\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Each correction becomes its \emph{own} episode. LeRobot indexes video per
episode and the policy consumes action chunks, so splicing two corrections
together would invent a transition that never happened.

\begin{lstlisting}[style=sh,caption={Mixture --- ratios must be explicit}]
MIX="${ORIG}@0.60:${HUMAN}@0.30:${AUTO}@0.10"
\end{lstlisting}

\warn{\textbf{Without explicit ratios the loader weights by dataset size.} The
corrections are 6{,}765 frames against the original's 55{,}701 --- they would
land at 11\,\% of the mix instead of 30\,\%. Confirm the \texttt{[mixture]} line
in the launch log shows three specs at the ratios you asked for.}

\subsection{Warm start, not resume}

The released checkpoint carries \textbf{weights only} --- no optimizer state, and
its cosine LR is annealed to ${\sim}3\times10^{-13}$. Continuation is therefore a
warm start with a fresh schedule, at a lower LR than the original run.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{base run} & \textbf{DAgger continuation}\\
\midrule
learning rate      & $1\times10^{-4}$ & $3\times10^{-5}$\\
global batch       & 128 & 128\\
frozen             & LLM + visual & same\\
tuned              & projector + diffusion head & same\\
\texttt{state\_dropout\_prob} & 0.2 & 0.2\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{How many steps}

Scale by \emph{exposure}, not by wall-clock. What matters is how often each
dataset is replayed:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{steps} & \textbf{original} & \textbf{corrections} & \textbf{time (2$\times$A100)}\\
\midrule
1{,}500 & 2.1$\times$ & 8.5$\times$  & 0.4\,h\\
6{,}000 & 8.3$\times$ & 34$\times$   & 1.5\,h\\
80{,}000 & 110$\times$ & \textbf{454$\times$} & 20\,h\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\note{A base run of 30{,}000 steps at batch 128 reached 68.9 epochs over the
original data without degrading, so this architecture tolerates high repetition
--- diffusion training injects noise every step and state dropout adds more. But
replaying 39 corrections from one fixed scene 454 times is memorisation, not
learning. Save often and pick by measurement.}

\subsection{Pick the checkpoint by measurement}

\warn{\textbf{In-training evaluation is not available.} The sharded dataset
factory asserts \texttt{eval\_strategy == "no"}; setting anything else kills the
run at startup. Checkpoint selection is therefore strictly post-hoc --- and
\textbf{not by loss}, because a handful of corrections are easy to memorise and
training loss keeps falling after held-out performance turns.}

Evaluate in \emph{Cartesian} space, not joint space. A 7-DoF arm reaches the same
point many ways, so a policy can be degrees off per joint and land correctly ---
or a degree off and miss by centimetres.

\subsection{What each level of testing actually proves}

\note{\textbf{Be precise about this.} A successful offline metric is not evidence
of robot behaviour.
\\[1mm]\hspace*{4mm}static $<$ offline eval $<$ open-loop $<$ closed-loop $<$ hardware\\[1mm]
Open-loop error in particular \emph{overstates} closed-loop failure: errors
accumulate unchecked because the policy never sees the consequences of its own
actions. On the robot it re-observes every step and corrects.}

\warn{\textbf{And a caution specific to HG-DAgger.} Held-out evaluation scores
agreement with the \emph{original demonstrations} --- but a correction is
\emph{by definition} a disagreement with them. A policy that learned your
corrections well can score \emph{worse} on that metric. Only closed-loop hardware
separates ``learned the corrections'' from ``got worse''.}

\section{Glossary}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0mm,style=nextline]
\item[HG-DAgger] Human-Gated DAgger. The policy runs; the human decides when to
take over. Only the intervened frames are treated as expert data, and they are
\emph{aggregated} with the original demonstrations rather than replacing them.
\item[Action chunk] The block of future actions the policy emits per inference.
40 frames here --- which is why a shorter correction yields nothing.
\item[Latched topic] A subscriber receives the current value on connect. Used
for state that must be correct even for a late joiner.
\item[Relative-delta teleop] Mode 2. Commands the follower by the leader's
\emph{change} since engage, not to the leader's absolute pose.
\item[Open-loop evaluation] Replaying recorded observations through the policy
without letting it affect the robot. Errors accumulate; it overstates failure.
\end{description}
